      The instrument

      An 18.8 tesla magnet, opened up

      The field comes from a superconducting solenoid of niobium alloys, held at 4.2 K in liquid helium and shielded by a jacket of liquid nitrogen and vacuum. Once energized it runs persistently for years with no power supply. Room-temperature shim coils in the bore flatten the field to better than a part per billion over the sample.

      The probe slides up into the bore from below. Its RF coil wraps the 5 mm tube and is tuned to three frequencies at once, so ¹H, ¹³C and ¹⁵N pulses can each be applied. Each line of the sequence diagram is one of these channels. A cryoprobe cools the coil and preamplifier to about 20 K, which roughly quadruples the signal-to-noise.

      How pulses move magnetization between nuclei

      Field notes for the simulator above. Every state it shows is computed with the product-operator formalism; the TOCSY transfer is a full spin-system calculation.

      01Pulses rotate, delays let couplings act

      An RF pulse at one nucleus' Larmor frequency rotates only that nucleus' magnetization: a 90° pulse tips it from z into the transverse plane, a 180° pulse inverts it. Between pulses two things happen. Each spin precesses at its chemical shift, and each pair of bonded spins evolves under their scalar coupling J. Coupling is the only way magnetization can move from one nucleus to another.

      02INEPT: the basic transfer step

      Start with ¹H magnetization, Hₓ. During a delay τ the coupling to ¹⁵N splits it into two vectors, one for each ¹⁵N spin state, which fan apart at ±J/2. After 1/(2J), 5.4 ms for ¹JNH ≈ 92 Hz, they point in opposite directions: the antiphase state 2HₓNz. A 180° pulse in the middle refocuses the chemical shift so only J acts. Then simultaneous 90° pulses on ¹H and ¹⁵N turn 2HzNz into 2HzNy, which is coherence on nitrogen. Because ¹H has 10× the gyromagnetic ratio of ¹⁵N, the nitrogen starts with 10× more polarization than it would have on its own.

      Try it: in the HSQC, step through the first INEPT and watch the two vectors fan apart in the vector picture.

      03The HSQC

      INEPT out to ¹⁵N, let the nitrogen chemical shift evolve during t₁, then reverse INEPT back to ¹H for detection. Every backbone NH gives one peak at (δH, δN), except the N-terminal Met1 and the three prolines (P19, P37, P38), which have no amide proton. The pairs of peaks at 6.8–7.6 ppm ¹H that share one ¹⁵N are the side-chain NH₂ groups of Asn and Gln.

      04HNCA and HN(CO)CA: walking the backbone

      In the HNCA the nitrogen of residue i couples to its own Cα (¹J ≈ 11 Hz) and to the Cα of residue i−1 through the carbonyl (²J ≈ 7 Hz). Both transfers happen at once, so each NH strip shows two Cα peaks, with the residue's own peak stronger. The HN(CO)CA routes the transfer through the carbonyl (¹⁵N→¹³C′→¹³Cα), which reaches only Cα(i−1). Comparing the two tells you which peak belongs to the preceding residue. Matching it to the strong peak in the neighbouring strip links the residues one by one.

      Try it: choose HNCA, then step through the residues with the arrows. The dashed line connects each residue's Cα(i−1) peak to its neighbour's own Cα. At a proline the walk breaks, because the proline has no NH strip.

      05TOCSY: spreading through the side chain

      During isotropic mixing, a continuous train of pulses (DIPSI-2) removes the chemical-shift differences between protons, so the ³J couplings mix them like coupled pendulums. Magnetization flows from Hα into the β, γ and δ protons and back to HN over tens of milliseconds. The amount reaching each proton depends on the coupling network. The HN–Hα coupling follows the backbone angle φ (Karplus relation), so it is large in β-strands and small in the helix.

      Try it: compare Lys6 (strand) with Ile23 (helix) and move the mixing-time slider.

      06Why 800 MHz

      Chemical shift differences in Hz grow linearly with the field, so peaks that overlap at 500 MHz separate at 800 MHz. Sensitivity grows roughly as B₀^3/2. For large proteins, TROSY variants exploit the cancellation of relaxation mechanisms, which works best around 900 MHz for amide groups.

      07What this simulator simplifies

      Pulses are ideal and instantaneous, relaxation is ignored, and gradients and phase cycles are modelled as filters that keep the wanted pathway. HNCA couplings are set to typical values (11 and 7 Hz) for every residue. In the TOCSY calculation methyl groups are treated as single spins, vicinal couplings are 7 Hz except HN–Hα (Karplus, from the 1UBQ φ angles), and geminal couplings are −14 Hz. Linewidths and peak heights are idealized.
